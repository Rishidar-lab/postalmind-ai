## FULL LINKEDIN POST

WHAT GDS ACTUALLY NEED

Closing this series' first run the way it should close: with proposals grounded in what the previous
posts actually established, not a generic list of grievances.

— **Written, transparent target instructions.** If a target exists, its basis and expectation should be
in writing and available to the person carrying it — not verbal, not retroactive.
— **Locality-sensitive target expectations.** A branch with different footfall and economic activity
should not be held to an identical target as a matter of routine.
— **An explicit prohibition on public humiliation.** Performance review in a group channel, by name, is
not the same as performance review — and should be named as a separate, prohibited category, not left
to informal norms.
— **Clear rules on after-hours communication.** Not a ban on all contact — a stated boundary, and a
record when it's crossed.
— **Transparent leave decisions**, documented and separable from target performance — leave approval
should never be a lever.
— **A firm separation between business-performance review and personal intimidation.** One is
supervision. The other isn't, regardless of the target attached to it.
— **A confidential grievance channel** that doesn't route a complaint about a supervisor through that
same supervisor.
— **Documented escalation** — a paper trail for how a serious complaint actually moved, not just its
outcome.
— **Independent review of serious complaints** — someone outside the immediate reporting line looking
at the record, not adjudicating it informally.
— **Stronger public source-transparency for GDS rules and circulars** — the documents this whole series
relies on should be easier for a GDS to find and cite for themselves, not mediated through a project
like this one.
— **Better public explanation of GDS status and role** — this entire series exists because that
explanation currently doesn't happen anywhere else at this level of detail. That gap shouldn't need an
independent project to fill it.

None of this is unrealistic, and none of it requires resolving the harder disputes (pay parity,
cadre status) to be worth doing on its own. Most of it is process, not policy — the kind of change that
doesn't need a committee report to justify, only the will to write it down and mean it.

This is not a campaign against individual postal employees.

It is a campaign to document the system.

Ground reality. Verified.

## RISK / QUALIFICATION NOTES
- Every proposal must read as PostalMind AI's own position ("PostalMind proposes," "worth discussing"),
  never as an existing Department commitment or union demand already agreed — that attribution would be
  fabricated.
- Avoid absolute language ("must," "will") about what the Department "should" do in a way that reads as
  asserting an obligation already established in law — these are policy proposals, not a legal claim.
- Keep the list specific enough to be falsifiable/actionable — a reform proposal so vague it can't be
  checked against isn't worth publishing under this project's standard.
