## FULL LINKEDIN POST

THE TARGET QUESTION

Let's be precise, because this is the point where a lot of commentary stops being credible.

Business target ≠ harassment.

Small-savings, RPLI/PLI and IPPB business targets are a documented, formally recognised part of GDS
work, tied to an incentive structure — not an informal add-on invented by a local officer. Having a
target, being reviewed against it, being asked to improve — none of that is, by itself, workplace
harassment. Saying otherwise would be exactly the kind of overclaim this project exists to avoid.

The real question is narrower and harder to dodge:

**When does a legitimate performance objective become coercive workplace pressure?**

That concern is not fringe. An MP raised it with the Ministry of Communications on behalf of GDS
employees; the Department's reported response was that no undue pressure should be placed on GDS for
target achievement, and that field officers have been advised accordingly. Whatever your view of how
well that instruction reaches every branch, its existence tells you the underlying concern was taken
seriously enough to require a written answer — not that it is resolved.

PostalMind's evidence tool does not try to answer "is this harassment" with a single score. It
classifies specific, checkable indicators separately, because they carry different weight:

— repeated individual demands, beyond a routine review cycle
— peer comparison — being ranked or compared against colleagues by name
— public naming — performance called out in a group channel, not a private review
— humiliation — language aimed at the person, not the number
— leave linkage — leave approval tied to target performance
— inspection threats — inspection raised as a consequence of target shortfall, not as routine
— after-hours follow-up — contact outside working hours about targets
— retaliation references — consequences threatened for raising the pressure itself

One of these, once, in a review conversation, is not the same evidence picture as several of these,
repeated, in writing. That is the distinction a target-vs-pressure claim has to survive before
PostalMind treats it as more than an allegation — and it is why the tool asks for a submitted case
before publishing a case, not the other way round.

This is not a campaign against individual postal employees.

It is a campaign to document the system.
