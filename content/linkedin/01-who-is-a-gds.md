# 01 — Who Is A GDS?

## TITLE
EVERYONE KNOWS THE POSTMAN. FEW PEOPLE KNOW THE GDS SYSTEM.

## HOOK

## FULL LINKEDIN POST — ENGLISH

Everyone knows the Postman.

Far fewer people understand the employment structure carrying a large part of rural postal service.

Gramin Dak Sevaks — GDS — run the branch post offices that reach villages a regular departmental post
office does not cover. They are part of the operational reality of rural India Post, not a footnote
to it.

But GDS engagement runs on its own framework — the Gramin Dak Sevaks (Conduct and Engagement) Rules,
2020 — separate from the rules covering a regular departmental Postman or Postwoman. Recruitment, the
allowance structure (a Time Related Continuity Allowance, not a regular pay scale), and service
conditions all follow this distinct track.

Documented GDS duties can include mail delivery and branch operations, savings-bank and financial
services, IPPB banking, small-savings and insurance (RPLI) business procurement, and general support
work at the branch. One person, several service lines, one engagement category most of the public has
never heard of.

That gap in public understanding is the starting point for this series. Not because the distinction
is a secret — it is written into the Department's own rules — but because almost nobody outside the
system is ever asked to know it.

PostalMind AI is beginning an evidence-led series, Ground Reality, to close that gap: what GDS
actually do, what the record says about their working conditions, and where the evidence is strong —
and where it is not.

This is not a campaign against individual postal employees.

It is a campaign to document the system.

Ground reality. Verified.

Independent project. Not affiliated with or endorsed by India Post or the Department of Posts.
